# Deepali Vyas: Visibility, your brand, and LinkedIn

When Deepali Vyas left Korn Ferry after seven years, she credited one thing above all with opening new doors: visibility. In this final episode of our unofficial Deepali Vyas series, we'll look at why she says visibility beats performance, why likability matters even though it's unfair, and her practical advice for building a personal brand on LinkedIn before you need it.

## Visibility beats performance

First, her bluntest claim. On the 10x Recruiting podcast, the themes of her conversation included that visibility beats performance every time, and that likability is a career accelerant, even if that's unfair. She doesn't present these as how things should be. She presents them as how things are, based on twenty-five years of watching who gets hired and promoted.

Her reasoning is simple. Decisions are made by people, and people can only choose someone they know about. Excellent work that nobody sees doesn't enter the conversation. And when two candidates are similarly qualified, the one people enjoy working with, and trust, usually wins.

## Likability without being fake

Second, what likability means in her framing. It isn't about being a people-pleaser or hiding disagreement. Drawing on her executive presence work, it's about leaving people feeling seen: listening, using names, following up on what they told you, being generous with credit and introductions. She pairs it with the idea that warmth is a dial you adjust for each room, while your confidence stays constant. You can be likable and still be direct.

## Build your brand before you need it

Third, timing. One of her recurring lines is to build your personal brand before you need it. The worst time to start networking or posting on LinkedIn is the week after a layoff, when you need something from everyone. The best time is when you're doing well and have something to give. In her words from one newsletter: your network is your net worth. She also frames networking as survival, not just socialising.

She uses her own story as the example. Over a few years, she built a presence on LinkedIn, TikTok and her newsletter. That visibility, she says, led to a book, a podcast and new ventures, and made it possible to leave a big firm on her own terms.

## LinkedIn, step by step

Fourth, her practical LinkedIn advice, in four parts. She describes LinkedIn as your digital business card, one that can bring you job offers, speaking invitations and useful contacts without you asking.

Part one: optimise your profile. Your headline should go beyond your job title. Say who you help and how you add value. For example, rather than "Senior Medical Writer", something like "Medical writer helping oncology teams deliver clear, on-time regulatory submissions." Your About section should be a short personal pitch, told as a story that shows your expertise and impact. Use the Featured section for your biggest wins: articles, talks, press, or your best posts. And use a professional, high-quality photo and banner, because they build trust in seconds. In her hidden job market newsletter, she adds that recruiters can only find people whose profiles use specific titles, skills and outcomes, and that a headline with just your current title is a sign you're invisible.

Part two: post with purpose. Quality beats frequency, so post less if you need to. Share industry insights, career lessons and leadership observations. Use real stories rather than corporate language, because people respond to experience. And mix formats: text, carousels, and short videos.

Part three: engage and get noticed. Leave thoughtful comments on high-visibility posts in your field. Send personal messages rather than generic connection requests. Share and tag others, without spamming.

Part four: be consistent. Visibility is a long game. Showing up week after week is what builds credibility.

She also has a recent video on five things to do regularly to grow on LinkedIn, and runs a thirty-day LinkedIn challenge, which shows how central she believes the platform is.

## The market test

Fifth, a quick check from another issue, to see whether your brand is working. Has a recruiter contacted you in the last ninety days? Would someone searching your name understand your value within ten seconds? Could three people outside your company describe your strengths? If you answer no to two or more, she says, your value is invisible to the market, and that's worth fixing whether or not you plan to move.

## Closing the series

That brings our unofficial Deepali Vyas series to a close. Across these fifteen episodes, one theme kept returning: performance gets you into the room, but visibility, relationships, sponsorship and strategy decide what happens there. Her shorthand is to play chess, not checkers.

## Recap

To recap. Deepali Vyas argues that visibility beats performance and likability accelerates careers, not because it's fair, but because decisions are made by people who can only choose someone they know and trust. Build your brand and your network before you need them. And on LinkedIn, write a headline about who you help, tell real stories, engage thoughtfully, and show up consistently.
